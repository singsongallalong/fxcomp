# Plasma echoes

Two sets of fine bands appear at different times and fade. After a quiet interval a third, potentially much broader band emerges. Its spacing and delay contain information about both earlier disturbances.

**Structure.** Collisionless streaming winds a perturbation into fine structure in velocity. Integrating over velocity can leave almost no density contrast. A second perturbation couples to that structure. In the idealized two-pulse temporal echo with wavenumbers $0<k_1<k_2$ at times $0$ and $\tau$, the difference mode $k_2-k_1$ rephases near $t=k_2\tau/(k_2-k_1)$. Closer spatial frequencies imply a broader, later echo. Collisions can attenuate it.

**Clock and coupling.** Density contrast can fall twice and then rise. The quiet interval has a history in phase space. Independent fading layers can imitate a particular clip, while the difference frequency and timing tie a family of such clips together. The visual conjecture is that recognizing the ancestry of the third band can give the preceding quiet a second meaning.

**Sources.** Gould, O’Neil and Malmberg (1967) predicted the echo. O’Neil and Gould (1968) analyze temporal and spatial echoes. Malmberg et al. (1968) report experimental observation.
