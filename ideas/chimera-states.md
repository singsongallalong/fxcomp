# Chimera states

On one arc of a ring, fixed neighbors keep their places in the beat. Elsewhere an individual gains a cycle, slips past its neighbors, and rejoins the crowd of drifting phases. A broad region can stay recognizable while its internal arrangement continually changes.

**Structure.** Identical phase oscillators with translation-invariant nonlocal coupling and a phase lag can sustain coexisting locked and drifting populations. The interaction range and phase response enter the existence of the state. An exactly synchronous state need not spontaneously leave synchrony under a symmetric deterministic evolution.

**Clock and coupling.** A spatially averaged complex phase describes how strongly an oscillator’s neighborhood is locked. Each oscillator contributes to that same field. Local coherence describes the partition and can remain statistically steady. One local slip is visible in a short clip. Establishing persistent coexistence takes repeated beats.

**Source.** Abrams and Strogatz (2004) solved a ring model with a cosine coupling kernel. [Chimera States for Coupled Oscillators](https://arxiv.org/abs/nlin/0407045)
