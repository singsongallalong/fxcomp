# Return-point memory

A dark patch expands in uneven jumps as a drive rises. On the way back its edge follows a different sequence. At a previous turning point, even a small crooked inlet regains its old outline.

**Structure.** In the zero-temperature ferromagnetic random-field Ising model under a quasistatic scalar drive, cooperative interactions and the no-passing property support return-point memory. Closing a properly nested loop restores the configuration at its enclosing reversal point. A sufficiently large excursion erases the intervening inner reversals.

**Clock and coupling.** The drive can have the same value on many distinct branches. The surviving hierarchy of extrema gives a better account of the state. Individual flips can trigger avalanches, and cycles dissipate even when their endpoints agree microscopically in the model. In a short clip, recognition of a single distinctive inlet could show a return. Demonstrating a hierarchy would take several excursions.

**Source.** Sethna et al. (1993) relate metastable states, avalanches and hierarchical memory. [Hysteresis and Hierarchies](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.70.3347)
