# Dispersive revivals

An uneven notch disperses into fine interference. Later several displaced copies of the same notch briefly line up across the field. The surviving asymmetry lets the eye recognize each copy’s ancestry.

**Structure.** For free periodic Schrödinger evolution on a circle the modal frequencies scale as the squares of the integer mode indices. At rational fractions of a revival period, their phase factors are periodic in the mode index. The field is then a finite complex-weighted sum of translates of its initial profile. Overlapping copies interfere, so the intensity image need not look like separated replicas.

**Clock and coupling.** Unitary evolution preserves modal magnitudes. The image is sensitive to their relative phases and can recover a motif without a second input. The time of return is encoded in the spectrum. This offers a counterpart to the plasma echo, whose timing also depends on the intervening perturbation.

**Source.** Olver, Sheils and Smith (2020) give revival and regularity results and analyze how the boundary conditions enter. [Revivals and Fractalisation in the Linear Free Space Schrödinger Equation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.08637)
