# Internal-wave attractors

A broad band of motion gradually outlines a polygon inside a basin. Its corners fall on the walls. The striking feature is the empty space on either side of the selected route.

**Structure.** In a uniformly stratified fluid, a monochromatic internal wave has a propagation angle fixed by frequency and stratification. Reflection at a sloping wall can narrow a beam. Composing such reflections around a suitable basin gives a contracting return map on ray positions and selects a repeated path.

**Clock and coupling.** Reflection count and transverse beam width measure the approach locally. The boundary couples successive propagation directions. The spatial focusing has a fine-scale counterpart in the wave field, and viscosity can limit the beam width. An attractor of the ray-position map by itself establishes no entropy law for the full wave. In a brief clip the broad-to-narrow transition is more informative than a polished final polygon.

**Source.** Maas, Benielli, Sommeria and Lam (1997) observed internal-wave focusing in a confined stratified fluid.
