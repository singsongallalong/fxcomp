# Foam coarsening and changes of neighbors

A small seven-sided cell grows beside a larger five-sided one. A six-sided cell can keep its area while every individual edge moves. An edge vanishes and the neighboring cells suddenly face different prospects.

**Structure.** For an interior cell in an ideal dry planar foam, von Neumann’s law gives $\dot A=K(n-6)$, where $n$ counts its sides. The fixed junction angles let the integrated curvature collapse to a side count. In wetter or quasi-two-dimensional foams additional geometric quantities enter.

**Clock and coupling.** Gas transfer across shared films couples neighbors. At late stages a typical cell size can give an ensemble age. A particular cell can change its side count and reverse its area trend. Coarsening can make an image look increasingly orderly while the system dissipates. In a short view, one exchange of neighbors can reveal the rule more clearly than an average over the whole mosaic.

**Source.** Chieco and Durian (2021) measured growth of individual bubbles and tested the range of the dry-foam law. [Experimentally Testing a Generalized Coarsening Model for Individual Bubbles in Quasi-Two-Dimensional Wet Foams](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.103.012610)
