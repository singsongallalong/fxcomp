# Heteroclinic switching

A field of nearly parallel rolls seems to settle. Across it a faint second grain develops. When the field turns over, the direction of the new rolls is the direction already present in that faint grain.

**Structure.** A symmetry-constrained amplitude system can have an attracting heteroclinic cycle joining saddle states. One roll family dominates near each saddle. A perturbation in an outgoing unstable direction grows and determines which family takes over. Near an ideal attracting cycle the residence times can lengthen on repeated visits. Perturbations and noise can limit the observable dwell times.

**Clock and coupling.** Competing modes regulate one another’s growth. The amplitude of the outgoing mode gives a local clock for departure. The same level of overall contrast recurs at different stops in the itinerary. One pause and takeover can convey forewarning in a short clip, while the claim about lengthening visits needs several.

**Sources.** Busse and Heikes (1980) compare a rotating convection model with observations. Mullowney, Julien and Meiss (2008) discuss roll switching, the amplitude model and its consequences for advection.
