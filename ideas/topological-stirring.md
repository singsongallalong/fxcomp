# Topological stirring

Three moving obstacles return to their starting positions. A material stripe threaded among them comes back with extra folds. The obstacles can keep an almost ceremonially simple rhythm while the stripe gets harder to trace.

**Structure.** In planar advection the spacetime braid of the obstacles constrains the mapping class of the period map. A pseudo-Anosov braid gives an exponential lower bound on the growth of lengths of essential material loops under iteration. Different regions can still mix at very different rates.

**Clock and coupling.** The influence runs from obstacle paths to flow to tracer. Incompressible passive advection conserves the tracer’s fine-scale distribution. Stripe width relative to the eye’s resolution predicts the apparent fade. With diffusion, the growing gradients accelerate the decay of tracer variance. The same sequence of pleats can therefore precede either apparent or dissipative erasure.

**Sources.** Boyland, Aref and Stremler (2000) connect braid type to stirring and demonstrate a three-stirrer experiment. Mathew, Mezić and Petzold (2005) quantify mixing across observation scales.
