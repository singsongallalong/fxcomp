# Geometric phase from a loop of shapes

A contour flexes through a few asymmetric bulges. When its familiar notch returns to its original place on the outline, the whole outline has shifted relative to the background. Both the returning notch and the external frame matter for seeing the displacement.

**Structure.** For a force- and torque-free swimmer in Stokes flow, the current shape and shape velocity determine body velocity through a connection on shape space. A closed stroke can have nontrivial holonomy in the rigid-motion group. In the quasistatic Stokes model the same oriented stroke produces the same net pose change under a change of traversal speed. Reversing it gives the inverse rigid motion.

**Clock and coupling.** The accumulated pose remembers the oriented path. Viscous work is dissipated even though reversing a stroke reverses its geometric displacement. This separates a physical arrow of time from a reversible feature played out within it. The two shape coordinates are particularly expressive together because their relative timing changes the loop.

**Source.** Shapere and Wilczek (1989) derive the connection and calculate net displacements for shape cycles. [Geometry of Self-Propulsion at Low Reynolds Number](https://doi.org/10.1017/S002211208900025X)
